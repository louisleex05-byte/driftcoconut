## Quick facts

- **Airport:** Phuket International Airport (**HKT**)
- **Currency:** Thai baht (**THB / ฿**). At the time of research, US$1 was about **THB 33.2**; use baht as your main reference because exchange rates move. [Currency converter](https://pluang.com/en/tools/currency-converter/usd-thb)
- **Time zone:** Indochina Time (**ICT, UTC+7**). Thailand does not use daylight saving time.
- **Language:** Thai is the working language. English is common in hotels, tour desks, restaurants, and beach areas, but less consistent in local transport and markets.
- **Visa note:** This is generally a **visa-exemption** destination, not a visa-on-arrival one, for many visitors. From **15 September 2026**, eligible US, UK, EU, Australian, and Japanese passport holders are generally covered by a 30-day visa-exemption framework. South Korean visitors may retain a separate 90-day bilateral arrangement. Check with a Thai embassy before you book, because the rules changed during 2026. [Visa update](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/se-asia/thailand-halves-visa-free-stays-to-30-days-for-passport-holders-from-60-places-including-singapore?ref=latest)
- **Mid-range daily spend:** Plan on **THB 4,300–6,600 (US$130–200)** per person for a comfortable hotel room, three meals, and local transport. Day trips come on top. [Currency converter](https://pluang.com/en/tools/currency-converter/usd-thb)
- **Local reality:** Phuket has no BTS, MRT, or metro. Your practical transport network is Smart Bus, songthaews, Grab, Bolt, taxis, hotel shuttles, and pre-booked cars.

## When to go

Phuket stays warm all year, with daytime highs generally sitting around 31–34°C. The real decision is not heat—it is rain, sea conditions, crowds, and what you are willing to pay for a room close to the beach.

**January and February** are the easy months. January averages 23.9–32.4°C with around 23.3 mm of rain over 4.2 days; February is similarly dry at 24.3–33.3°C with around 25.8 mm over three rain days. You get clearer beach days and more dependable island boats, but hotels around Patong, Kata, Karon, and Bang Tao are busy and expensive. Reserve early if you are travelling around New Year or school holiday dates. [Weather data](https://worldweather.wmo.int/en/city.html?cityId=579&ref=rawai.com)

**March** turns hotter and stickier, reaching about 33.8°C, while rain begins to return. It is still a good beach month, though prices can stay high. Makha Bucha falls on **3 March 2026**, when alcohol-sale restrictions may affect restaurant and bar plans. [Thailand holiday calendar](https://kulibrary.wordpress.com/2025/09/03/2026-thailands-official-holidays/)

**April** is hot, humid, and lively. Expect around 25.3–33.7°C, more showers, and a sharp rise in demand around **Songkran, 13–15 April 2026**. Phuket’s water celebrations can begin around 12 April, and traffic becomes slow around Patong and the main beach roads. Book your hotel and airport transfer before the holiday begins. [Weather data](https://worldweather.wmo.int/en/city.html?cityId=579&ref=rawai.com)

From **May through October**, the southwest monsoon changes the rhythm. May brings roughly 269.8 mm of rain; June and July remain wet; August stays unsettled; September is statistically the wettest month, with about 386.5 mm across 22.8 rain days. Prices fall, hotel deals become easier to find, and the island feels less crowded. The compromise is rougher seas, red flags on west-coast beaches, and boat trips that may be changed or cancelled. Build one spare day into any Phi Phi, Phang Nga Bay, or diving plan. [Weather data](https://worldweather.wmo.int/en/city.html?cityId=579&ref=rawai.com)

**October** is still wet, though the Phuket Vegetarian Festival brings a particular intensity to Phuket Town. Published 2026 listings place it around **11–19 October**, but this should be reconfirmed locally because the festival follows lunar-calendar timing. [Thailand festivals](https://www.thaiholidayguide.com/events/festivals-in-thailand/)

By **November**, rain begins to ease and room prices rise again. Loy Krathong is listed as **24 November 2026** by one source, while another calendar gives 25 November, so treat the exact local Phuket programme as [UNCERTAIN] until closer to the date. December is firmly peak season: rainfall falls to around 61.9 mm, but festive-season rooms are often the most expensive of the year. [Weather data](https://worldweather.wmo.int/en/city.html?cityId=579&ref=rawai.com)

Phuket is not northern Thailand’s main burning-season destination, but haze and local pollution can still affect sensitive travelers. If you have asthma, check the live AQI before planning a long beach walk, viewpoint drive, or outdoor boat day.

## Where to stay

### Patong

Patong is Phuket at its loudest: beach mornings, tour boats, shopping, late dinners, neon-lit Bangla Road, and the kind of convenience that makes a first trip simple. It suits travelers who want nightlife, a dense choice of restaurants, easy tour pickups, and a beach within walking distance of their hotel.

For a mid-range room, expect roughly **THB 1,800–4,000 (US$54–120)** in lower and shoulder periods, with much higher prices in December, January, and around Songkran. **The Bloc Hotel** is close to Patong Beach and Bangla Road, while **Lub d Phuket Patong** works well if you want a social hotel with a more casual feel. Current nightly rates are [UNCERTAIN] and should be checked against live booking inventory before publication.

From central Patong, you can walk to Patong Beach, Jungceylon, Bangla Road, seafood restaurants, massage shops, pharmacies, ATMs, exchange counters, and dozens of tour desks. The practical local transport point is the Patong Smart Bus stop and beach-road pickup zone; there is no rail station.

Patong is useful, but it is not quiet. If your room faces Bangla Road or a bar-heavy lane, expect sound late into the night.

### Kata and Karon

Kata and Karon make more sense when you want a beach base without the full volume of Patong. The streets here still have restaurants, cafés, massage shops, pharmacies, and convenience stores, but the evenings settle earlier. Kata has a compact, walkable feel; Karon gives you a longer beach and more space between hotels.

Typical mid-range rates sit around **THB 1,700–3,800 (US$51–114)**, depending on season and distance from the sand. **Chanalai Garden Resort, Kata Beach** is a useful option for a Kata stay, while **Sugar Marina Resort–ART–Karon Beach** is a mid-range reference point for Karon. You can walk to Kata Beach or Karon Beach, local markets, casual Thai restaurants, coffee shops, convenience stores, and sunset spots depending on your exact address. Kata and Karon have Smart Bus stops, but after dark Grab, Bolt, taxis, or a hotel transfer are usually more practical.

For a first Phuket trip, Kata is often the calmer choice: enough going on to avoid feeling isolated, without needing to sleep beside Patong’s nightlife.

### Phuket Old Town

Phuket Old Town is for slow mornings, local food, heritage streets, and late-afternoon walks under the old shophouse façades. It is not a beach neighborhood, but it gives you a more grounded version of the island than the resort strip.

A mid-range room usually lands around **THB 1,300–3,000 (US$39–90)**. **Casa Blanca Boutique Hotel** is a central boutique option, and **The Memory at On On Hotel** puts you near the historic core. Live pricing is [UNCERTAIN] because it changes with dates, weekends, and festivals.

From here, walk Thalang Road, Soi Romanee, the surrounding Sino-Portuguese lanes, cafés, shrines, independent restaurants, and the Sunday Walking Street when it is running. This is the right place to linger over a meal rather than rush between attractions. You are near the Phuket Town local-bus terminal, airport-bus connections, and songthaew routes—but not a metro or BTS station.

Old Town works particularly well for a first night after landing, a rainy-season base, or two nights before moving to the beach. Pair it with a west-coast hotel if you have enough time.

### Bang Tao, Laguna, and Cherngtalay

Bang Tao, Laguna, and Cherngtalay are spread-out, polished, and more resort-oriented. You come here for long beach walks, beach clubs, villas, Boat Avenue dinners, Porto de Phuket shopping, and the comfort of having good cafés nearby without Patong’s noise.

Mid-range rooms start around **THB 2,500–6,000 (US$75–181)**, while beachfront resorts and villas can move well beyond that in peak season. **Cassia Phuket** is a useful benchmark near Laguna, while **Best Western Premier Bangtao Beach Resort & Spa** gives you a beachfront mid-range reference. You can walk to parts of Bang Tao Beach, Boat Avenue, Porto de Phuket, cafés, restaurants, beach clubs, and resort facilities, though the district is larger than it looks on a map. The nearest practical transport is along the Bang Tao/Cherngtalay Smart Bus corridor. For dinners beyond your immediate area, use Grab, Bolt, a taxi, hotel shuttle, or a pre-booked driver.

This is a strong base for longer stays, resort time, remote work, and travelers prepared to spend more on transport.

## Things to do

### Half-day: Old Phuket Town on foot

Start early on Thalang Road, then move through Soi Romanee and the lanes around it. You can see Sino-Portuguese shophouses, shrines, cafés, murals, and local food stalls without paying an entry fee. A self-guided walk is free apart from food and transport; guided walks commonly cost **THB 500–1,200 (US$15–36)**. Go in the morning for quieter streets or in late afternoon when the heat softens. [Tour-price guide](https://travelguidestip.com/best-tours-phuket/)

### Half-day: Wat Chalong and the island viewpoints

Pair Wat Chalong with a viewpoint and a stop in Old Town. Dress with shoulders and knees covered, remove shoes before entering appropriate temple spaces, and avoid treating religious areas as a photo set. Group tours commonly cost **THB 800–1,500 (US$24–45)**; private tours run around **THB 3,000–6,000 (US$90–181)**. Check Big Buddha access locally before committing to a route.

## Getting around

Phuket is large, hilly in places, and not built around rail transport. Walking is useful inside Patong or Old Town, but not for crossing the island.

From Phuket International Airport, the **Smart Bus** is the budget choice at about **THB 100 (US$3)** per person on its route. A shared minivan to Patong often costs **THB 200–300 (US$6–9)** but may wait for passengers and stop at several hotels. Airport taxis, Grab, and Bolt commonly cost around **THB 600–1,050 (US$18–32)** to Patong, while a private transfer is roughly **THB 800–2,500+ (US$24–75+)**. [Airport transport guide](https://www.thetraveler.org/phuket-airport-transportation-guide-taxis-grab-smart-bus/)

Patong is around **45–60 minutes** from the airport in normal traffic; rain, Songkran, and evening congestion can make it longer. Grab estimates to Kata or Karon can reach **THB 950–1,300**, while Phuket Town often falls within a broad **THB 500–1,000** range depending on the service and time. [Transfer guide](https://www.southeastasiasimplified.com/blog/transfer-guides-5/phuket-airport-transfer-guide-all-options-2026-165)

## Local tips + scams

Keep the local basics simple: **head high, feet low**. Do not touch someone’s head, step over people, or point the soles of your feet at people, food, Buddha images, or monks. In temples and government facilities, cover your shoulders and knees. When you see shoes outside a home, small shop, or private premise, take yours off too.

The most avoidable money problems begin with rentals. Before taking a jet ski or scooter, film every panel, wheel, scratch, and dent while staff are present. Damage-claim disputes can become expensive fast. Avoid leaving your passport as a deposit if another option is available.

For transport, do not accept a vague “cheap taxi” offer without agreeing on the fare. Some drivers may say your hotel, tour, or attraction is closed and suggest another place instead. Check the venue yourself, then compare the quote with Grab or Bolt. Phuket taxi prices are usually higher than Bangkok, but you should still know the app fare before negotiating.

The third trap is a low-priced tour with expensive extras. A Phi Phi or Similan headline price may exclude park fees, transfers, snorkel gear, lunch, or insurance. Ask for the full inclusion list in writing before you pay. Phi Phi park fees are sometimes quoted separately at **THB 400 (US$12)**, while Similan park fees are commonly cited around **THB 300 (US$9)**. [Tour-price guide](https://travelguidestip.com/best-tours-phuket/)

One Phuket habit makes a big difference in the wet months: plan around the **morning weather window**. Do the beach, viewpoint, or boat trip when the day is clearest, then leave Old Town, cafés, massage, food, and indoor time for later. A forecast showing rain does not mean the whole day is lost.

And skip the “weird wildlife” challenge meals. Do not eat unfamiliar bugs or wild species simply for a video or story; Phuket has plenty of good food without turning dinner into a hospital visit.

## FAQ

### Is Phuket safe for solo female travelers?

Phuket can work well for solo female travelers when you use ordinary city-and-beach precautions. Take app rides or reputable transfer services after dark, avoid isolated roads and beaches at night, watch your drink, keep alcohol moderate, and choose accommodation with strong recent reviews. Traffic and water safety deserve as much attention as personal security.

### How many days do I need in Phuket?

Plan **four to five nights** for a first trip. That gives you an arrival day, a beach day, Old Town and Wat Chalong, one island or bay trip, and a spare weather day. Stay seven nights if you want to add Khao Sok, a liveaboard, diving, or a slower split stay between Old Town and the coast.

### Is Phuket expensive?

It can be. A backpacker can manage on around **THB 1,400–2,300 (US$42–69)** daily, while a comfortable mid-range trip is closer to **THB 4,300–6,600 (US$130–200)** before major excursions. Luxury starts around **THB 10,000–20,000+ (US$300–600+)** a day. Your hotel month and transport choices matter most. [Currency converter](https://pluang.com/en/tools/currency-converter/usd-thb)

### What is the best area for first-timers?

Choose **Kata** if you want a beach, restaurants, cafés, and a manageable evening scene without Patong’s late-night noise. Choose Patong if nightlife, centrality, and tour convenience are the point of the trip. Phuket Old Town is better for food and culture than beach access, while Bang Tao works best with a higher transport and hotel budget.

### Do I need cash in Phuket?

Yes, but you do not need to carry your whole travel budget in baht. Hotels, malls, better restaurants, and established tour operators often take cards. Small restaurants, markets, tips, independent drivers, beach vendors, and local services may prefer cash. Carry small notes, use an ATM when needed, and keep a card as backup.

### Can I swim in Phuket during the rainy season?

Sometimes, but only when conditions are clearly safe. From around May to October, west-coast beaches can have strong surf and rip currents. Follow red flags without debate, do not swim just because other people are in the water, and keep island-boat days flexible. September and October are particularly wet months.
