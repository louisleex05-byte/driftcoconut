## Quick facts

- **Airport:** Chiang Mai International Airport (**CNX**) sits about 4 km southwest of the Old City. [Source](https://www.byklo.rent/en/blog/first-time-visiting-chiang-mai-guide/)
- **Currency:** Thai baht (฿ / THB). For planning in September 2026, use roughly **฿33.15 = US$1**; the rate you receive at an ATM, exchange booth, or on your card can differ. [Source](https://pluang.com/en/tools/currency-converter/usd-thb)
- **Time:** ICT, UTC+7, all year. Thailand does not use daylight saving time.
- **Language:** Thai is the everyday language. Northern Thai, or Kham Mueang, remains part of the region's culture; English is common in hotels, tour businesses, malls, and tourist-facing restaurants.
- **Rail transit:** Chiang Mai has no BTS Skytrain or MRT. You will get around by walking, Grab, taxi, songthaew, tuk-tuk, local bus, or motorbike.
- **Mid-range spend:** Plan **US$90"“150 per person daily**, including a hotel around US$55, meals, and local transport. A reported citywide mid-range hotel median is around ฿1,950/US$55. [Source](https://travelguidestip.com/where-to-stay-chiang-mai/)
- **Entry note:** For US, UK, Australian, Japanese, and many EU passport holders, reported rules from September 1, 2026 provide visa-exempt"”not visa-on-arrival"”entry for up to 30 days. Check your Royal Thai Embassy or Thai Immigration guidance immediately before flying; rules can change. [Source](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/tourism/40070511)

## When to go

For the cleanest, coolest Chiang Mai, come between **November and January**. Days tend to sit around 28"“30°C and nights can drop to 14"“19°C. Rain is light"”November averages about 50 mm, December around 14 mm, and January around 7 mm"”so this is the time for temple walks, Doi Suthep viewpoints, and long evenings outside. It is also peak season, which means the higher end of hotel pricing and fewer last-minute rooms. [Source](https://www.beautiful-chiangmai.com/info/climate-of-chiang-mai/)

February begins pleasantly at around 32°C/15°C with only about 5 mm of rain, but haze can begin late in the month. March is hotter, roughly 35°C/19°C, very dry, and often the point when Chiang Mai's burning season becomes a serious trip-planning issue. April is hotter still"”around 36°C/22°C"”with Songkran on **April 13"“15, 2026** and again **April 13"“15, 2027**. The moat becomes part of the water fight, traffic slows, and demand rises briefly even though the broader hot-and-hazy season usually brings lower hotel rates. [Source](https://www.beautiful-chiangmai.com/info/climate-of-chiang-mai/) [Source](https://fengniii.com/en/archives/4913)

I would be careful about booking non-refundable stays from mid-February through April. In severe periods, PM2.5 can sit in the 100"“300 micrograms-per-cubic-metre range, and one 2026 episode reached 314.3. Check the Thai government's live readings on [Air4Thai](https://air4thai.pcd.go.th/webV3/#/Home) before you book and every morning while you are here. If the number is poor, keep the day flexible, use an N95/FFP2 mask, and skip hard outdoor exercise. [Source](https://chiangmai.guide/guides-hub/chiang-mai-weather-when-to-visit)

May brings pre-monsoon storms and about 158 mm of rain; June through September are wet, green, and cheaper. June averages around 32°C/23°C with roughly 130 mm of rain, July around 160 mm, August around 220 mm, and September about 230"“250 mm. I would avoid June through September if your trip depends on mountain driving, waterfalls, or a tight schedule: downpours, loose gravel, slower traffic, and occasional localized flooding can change the day quickly. [Source](https://www.thai-aec.com/chiang-mai-weather-by-month/) [Source](https://www.beautiful-chiangmai.com/info/climate-of-chiang-mai/)

Late October is the useful compromise: rain is easing, the countryside is still green, and rates are usually below peak-season levels. November then turns busy fast, especially for Yi Peng and Loy Krathong. In 2026, Yi Peng runs **November 24"“25** and Loy Krathong falls **November 24**; 2027 dates are expected to be **November 13"“14** and **November 13** respectively, pending local confirmation. Book hotels and ticketed lantern releases far ahead. [Source](https://fengniii.com/en/archives/4913)

## Where to stay

### Old City

Inside the moat is the most practical base for a first Chiang Mai trip: old brick walls, temple compounds, massage shops, coffee stops, and narrow lanes that make it easy to fill a day without arranging transport. It suits first-timers, families, culture-focused travelers, and anyone who wants to walk between **Wat Phra Singh**, **Wat Chedi Luang**, **Tha Phae Gate**, and the Sunday Walking Street area.

Expect mid-range rooms around **US$40"“100 a night**. **Rachamankha** is reported around **US$85"“110**, while **Tamarind Village** is around **US$90"“120**. Prices rise around Christmas, New Year, and the November lantern festival. [Source](https://travelguidestip.com/best-hotels-in-chiang-mai/)

There is no metro station. Think of the Old City as Chiang Mai's central pickup zone: Grab, tuk-tuks, songthaews, and tours all understand it, and an airport taxi takes roughly 15 minutes from CNX.

### Nimman / Nimmanhaemin

Nimman is where you stay for coffee roasters, shopping, coworking-friendly cafés, bars, and a more contemporary hotel scene. It works well if you are working remotely, returning to Chiang Mai after a previous temple-heavy visit, or want to be near **One Nimman**, **MAYA Lifestyle Shopping Center**, and the small streets off Nimmanhaemin Road.

A typical mid-range stay sits around **US$55"“95**. Published 2026 price references put **BED Nimman** around **US$55"“75** and **Kantary Hills** around **US$70"“95**. [Source](https://travelguidestip.com/best-hotels-in-chiang-mai/)

Nimman is close to the airport"”airport taxis are commonly estimated at **฿180"“220**"”but it has no rail station. Use Grab for the short ride to the Old City. And do not spend every meal inside the polished café strip: explore nearby local markets, or *kard*, for cheaper northern food and a more everyday pace. Chang Khlan is a convenient evening-first area beside the Night Bazaar, with easy access to stalls, food, and the Ping River side of town. Choose it for a short stay, family trip, or shopping-focused itinerary when being able to walk to **Chiang Mai Night Bazaar**, **Anusarn Market**, and **Kalare Night Bazaar** matters more than waking beside temples.

Use **US$40"“90 a night** as a working mid-range range, but treat it as a planning estimate rather than a fixed live quote. ### Ping River / Charoenrat / Riverside

The riverside is slower and more residential in feel, with riverfront restaurants, design shops, and more space between you and the Old City crowds. It suits couples, quieter stays, and travelers building in a few unhurried dinners near **Warorot Market** and the craft-and-café pockets around Charoenrat Road.

Mid-range riverside rooms run around **US$48"“120**, with higher pricing at luxury properties. **The Riverside Hotel** is reported around **US$48"“65**; **Diamond River Ping Hotel** is reported at about **฿800"“1,400** (roughly US$24"“42). [Source](https://travelguidestip.com/best-hotels-in-chiang-mai/) [Source](https://www.paglipat.com/en/hotels/no-extra-guest-fees/chiang-mai)

There is no train station here either. A taxi from CNX is usually **฿200"“250**, and Grab is the simplest way to connect with Old City temples or Nimman dinners. [Source](https://www.thetraveler.org/chiang-mai-airport-bus-guide-to-nimman-old-city-night-bazaar/)

## Local tips + scams

At temples, cover your shoulders and knees when requested, remove shoes before entering sacred indoor spaces, and do not point your feet toward Buddha images or monks. Keep your voice low inside temple grounds. Thailand's social cues are gentler than many visitors expect: do not touch someone's head, do not use your feet to move an object or point at a person, and try not to turn a small disagreement into a public performance.

The most common transport problem is not always a dramatic scam; it is unclear pricing. With a tuk-tuk or red songthaew, say the destination, total baht amount, and whether the fare is for the vehicle or each passenger before you board. If somebody offers a suspiciously cheap ride with "just one quick stop," you may be steered to shops that pay drivers commission. Decline and find another ride.

If you rent a motorbike, photograph and video the panels, mirrors, wheels, fuel gauge, and existing scratches before taking the key. Keep the rental paperwork. This gives you a record if a damage argument appears when you return it.

For food, Chiang Mai is generally easy to eat in, but I would stick to malls, restaurants, and known street stalls when you are new to the city. Skip unfamiliar wildlife dishes, raw meat, or risky novelty food if you do not want your itinerary interrupted by a hospital visit or a miserable night in your room.

My small local habit: use the moat roads to orient yourself, but do not make them your main walking route. Cut through the quiet temple lanes and small sois inside the Old City, then let your day finish near Tha Phae Gate or the Sunday Walking Street zone. It is less noisy, less exposed to traffic, and far better for finding a place to pause.

## FAQ

### Is Chiang Mai safe for solo female travelers?

Chiang Mai is often manageable for solo travel because the main visitor areas are compact and busy, but use ordinary city habits. Stay in a well-reviewed property, take Grab or a pre-agreed taxi after dark, avoid isolated roads, protect your drink, and use established operators for tours and rentals. Smoke season is the larger Chiang Mai-specific health consideration.

### How many days do I need in Chiang Mai?

Give yourself three or four full days. That covers an Old City temple day, Doi Suthep, one organised activity such as a cooking class or elephant sanctuary, and a day trip. Stay five to seven days if you want Doi Inthanon, a slower riverside day, café time in Nimman, or a weather buffer during the rainy season.

### Is Chiang Mai expensive?

Not by major-city standards, but your timing and neighbourhood matter. Backpackers can plan around **US$30"“55 daily**; mid-range travelers around **US$90"“150**; luxury stays around **US$220"“450+**. Dorms reportedly begin around US$12, while citywide mid-range hotel pricing is around US$55. November through January and Yi Peng week cost more. [Source](https://travelguidestip.com/best-hotels-in-chiang-mai/) [Source](https://travelguidestip.com/where-to-stay-chiang-mai/)

### What is the best area for first-time visitors?

Stay in the **Old City** if you want to walk to Wat Phra Singh, Wat Chedi Luang, Tha Phae Gate, food stalls, and the Sunday Walking Street zone. It also makes tour pickups simple. Choose Nimman only if cafés, nightlife, shopping, and a more modern hotel atmosphere matter more to you than immediate access to historic temples. [Source](https://www.byklo.rent/en/blog/first-time-visiting-chiang-mai-guide/)

### Do I need cash in Chiang Mai?

Yes"”carry cash, especially small notes. Hotels, malls, major restaurants, and many cafés take cards, but small street vendors, food stalls, red songthaews, tuk-tuks, temple donations, and some independent shops may not. Keep enough baht for a few meals and short rides rather than assuming contactless payment will work everywhere.

### When should I avoid Chiang Mai for air quality?

Be most cautious from **mid-February through April**, especially March and early April. Smoke can be severe even on a bright-looking day, and PM2.5 readings in bad periods have been reported in the 100"“300 range, including a 2026 episode at 314.3 micrograms per cubic metre. Check Air4Thai daily and keep bookings flexible. [Source](https://chiangmai.guide/guides-hub/chiang-mai-weather-when-to-visit)
