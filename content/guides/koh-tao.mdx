## Quick facts

- **Gateway:** Koh Tao has no airport. The fastest route is ferry from Chumphon (about 1h45m); it's also reachable via Koh Phangan or Koh Samui if you're island-hopping. [ferrysamui.com](https://www.ferrysamui.com/route-chumphon-tao-island.html)
- **Currency:** Thai baht (**B / THB**). Use around **B33.3 = US$1** for September 2026 planning, then check the live rate before exchanging.
- **Time:** ICT, UTC+7, no daylight saving time.
- **Language:** Thai is the everyday language; English is standard at dive shops, resorts, and Sairee Beach's restaurant strip.
- **Visa note:** From 15 September 2026, US, UK, Australian, Japanese, and listed EU passport holders can use Thailand's 30-day visa-exemption scheme for tourism, subject to immigration requirements.
- **Daily budget (non-diving):** Hostel dorms run **B250-450**, mid-range fan rooms **B700-1,200**, air-con private rooms **B1,200-2,500**. Street food is **B70-120** a meal, a beach restaurant meal **B250-450**. [siamscuba.com](https://siamscuba.com/blog/koh-tao-diving-cost-guide)

## When to go

November through April brings the calmest seas and the smoothest ferry crossings — the safest window if you get seasick easily or you're traveling with kids. [ferryhopper.com](https://www.ferryhopper.com/en/ferries/thailand/koh-tao)

Koh Tao's actual peak season runs March through September, driven by diving demand rather than beach weather — this is when dive shops are busiest and course availability gets tight, so book a course two months ahead if your dates fall in this window. [travelfish.org](https://www.travelfish.org/transport/thailand/southern_thailand/surat_thani/ko_tao/all)

October through December is the rougher-seas stretch, with a real chance of ferry delays or cancellations during storms — visibility for diving can also drop in this window. Shoulder months like May or January are the sweet spot if you want calmer seas, fewer crowds, and lower prices without hitting the worst of the monsoon.

## Where to stay

### Sairee Beach

Sairee is Koh Tao's main strip — the longest beach, the most dive shops, and the widest range of accommodation from hostel dorms to beachfront resorts. This is the default first-time base: everything you need is within walking distance, including most of the island's nightlife. ## Things to do

### Half-day

**Discover Scuba Diving** is the no-commitment intro dive for anyone unsure about a full course — a single supervised dive runs around **B2,600**, with an optional second dive the same day for about **B1,000** more. [siamscuba.com](https://siamscuba.com/blog/koh-tao-diving-cost-guide)

**John-Suwan Viewpoint** on the southern tip is a short, steep hike rewarding you with one of the island's best panoramic bay shots — best done in the early morning before the heat sets in.

<GuidePhoto slot="viewpoint" />

### Full-day

**PADI Open Water Diver certification** is Koh Tao's signature activity — a three-day course covering theory, confined-water practice, and four open-water dives, typically priced **B9,900-12,000 (roughly US$285-345)** including gear, boat trips, and certification fees. Shop around between dive centers; quality and group size vary more than price does. [blackturtledive.com](https://www.blackturtledive.com/diving-course-prices/)

<GuidePhoto slot="activity" />

**Certified fun dives** for those already licensed run **B1,000-1,800** per dive — a good way to explore Koh Tao's reefs without committing to a new course.

### Multi-day

**Advanced Open Water or Rescue Diver courses** are the natural next step for certified divers with a few days to spare — Koh Tao's dive shops are dense enough that you can typically start within a day or two of arriving, unlike more remote dive destinations.

**Island hop to Koh Phangan or Koh Samui** to close out the trip with less diving and more nightlife or beach time — ferries run multiple times daily in each direction.

## Getting around

From Chumphon, the fastest ferry service takes about 1 hour 45 minutes to Koh Tao. [ferrysamui.com](https://www.ferrysamui.com/route-chumphon-tao-island.html) From Koh Phangan's Thong Sala Pier, the crossing to Mae Haad Pier takes roughly one to one-and-three-quarter hours, with tickets around **B450-650**. [backpackerswanderlust.com](https://www.backpackerswanderlust.com/koh-phangan-to-koh-tao/)

On the island, songthaews connect Mae Haad, Sairee, and Chalok along the main roads; scooter rental is common for reaching quieter bays, though several of Koh Tao's roads are steep enough that inexperienced riders should stick to songthaews or arranged transport instead.

## Local tips + scams

Scooter rental damage disputes are a known issue here — photograph the bike from every angle before you ride off, and use an established shop with a written agreement rather than the cheapest roadside stall.

For diving, check that your chosen shop is a genuine PADI (or SSI) center with visible certification — the island has enough dive shops that quality varies, and a good instructor matters more for both safety and enjoyment than a slightly lower course price.

As with the rest of Thailand: dress modestly at temples, don't point your feet at people or Buddha images, and agree songthaew fares before getting in.

## FAQ

### Do I need to already know how to dive?

No — most first-time visitors arrive with zero diving experience and leave with a PADI Open Water certification. Discover Scuba Diving is the lower-commitment option if you're not sure yet.

### How many days do I need?

Budget at least four days if you're doing the Open Water course (three days of instruction plus a buffer). A pure beach visit without diving can work in two to three nights.

### Is Koh Tao expensive?

Diving is the main cost driver — outside course fees, daily living costs (food, basic accommodation) are comparable to or slightly cheaper than Koh Samui or Koh Phangan.

### Is Koh Tao good for non-divers?

Yes, though it's built around diving culture — beaches, hiking to viewpoints, and a quieter atmosphere than Koh Phangan make it workable for a non-diving stay, just know the vibe skews toward the dive-shop crowd.
