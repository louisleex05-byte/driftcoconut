## Quick facts

* **Airport:** Hat Yai International Airport (HDY), located 12 km southwest of central Hat Yai.
* **Currency & Rate:** Thai Baht (THB, ฿). Current conversion sits around $1 USD ≈ 33.20 THB (1 THB ≈ $0.030 USD).
* **Time Zone:** Indochina Time (ICT, UTC+7). Thailand does not observe Daylight Saving Time.
* **Primary Languages:** Central Thai, Southern Thai (*Pasa Tai*), Southern Thai Hokkien and Teochew Chinese dialects, and Southern Thai Malay. English and Bahasa Malaysia are common across downtown retail desks.
* **Visa Status (2026):** Travelers holding passports from the US, UK, EU member states, Australia, Japan, and South Korea enter on the 60-Day Visa Exemption Scheme. You can extend this once for 30 days at Songkhla Immigration without paying a Visa-on-Arrival fee.
* **Daily Mid-Range Cost:** Plan on spending $52 to $75 USD per day (covering a private room, three full meals, and local hops via ride-share or songthaew).

## When to go

Hat Yai and Songkhla follow the Gulf of Thailand’s dual-monsoon rhythm. Forget the typical Andaman calendar that dictates Phuket or Krabi; down here, the rain patterns take their own sweet time.

January brings daytime temperatures between 21°C and 31°C with roughly 88 mm of rainfall, marking a cool, breezy shoulder season as the fierce northeast winds let up. February steps in as the driest month of the year (23°C–33°C, ~30 mm rain). It is the sweet spot for coastal walking along Songkhla’s shoreline without ducking under awnings every twenty minutes. March heats up quickly to 34°C with around 77 mm of rain, drawing cross-border weekenders down for early market mornings.

April is the hottest stretch of the year (25°C–35°C, ~116 mm rain) and brings intense, quick afternoon downpours alongside the Hat Yai Midnight Songkran on April 12–14, 2026 (returning April 12–14, 2027). The city center closes down Niphat Uthit 3 Road for after-dark water fights that sell out downtown hotel beds months ahead. May through July transitions into a quieter, lower-season pattern where heat stays high around 34°C–35°C, rainfall hovers between 100 mm and 128 mm, and hotel rates drop by 20% to 30% on weekdays.

August and September see increased humidity (24°C–33°C, 115 mm to 140 mm rain). This stretch carries a unique regional caveat: transboundary peat smoke blown across the Malacca Strait from Sumatra, Indonesia. When these seasonal southwest winds align with dry burns, PM2.5 can spike above 80 µg/m³.

The serious skies open in late autumn. October brings the start of the true monsoon with 220 mm of rain, though it also hosts the street processions of the Hat Yai Vegetarian Festival (Tesagan Gin Je) from October 10–19, 2026 (September 30–October 9, 2027) and Songkhla’s Chak Phra lake procession on October 26, 2026 (October 15, 2027). November is the wettest month by a mile: 320 mm of rain falls amid 23°C–30°C temperatures, causing street-level flash pooling in low-lying basin areas. Loy Krathong wraps up on the water on November 24, 2026 (November 13, 2027). By December, the deluge slowly winds down (~240 mm), but holiday bookings from Malaysia create price surges of 40% to 60% across downtown properties, overriding the wet weather.

## Where to stay

### Central Downtown Hat Yai (Lee Gardens / Niphat Uthit 1–3)

This is the city's neon-streaked commercial anchor, packed wall-to-wall with long-running Hokkien dim sum parlors, night market stalls, cotton tailors, and late-night foot reflexology rooms. It suits independent travelers, shoppers, and street-food lovers who want to step straight out of the lobby and walk to dinner without thinking about a ride. You will find solid mid-range private rooms running from $28 to $45 USD per night. The long-standing Centara Hotel Hat Yai sits right around $44 USD a night, while Lee Gardens Plaza Hotel puts you right above the pedestrian action for roughly $28 USD. From here, you can walk straight to the dry goods at Kim Yong Market, the outdoor racks at Suntisuk Market, and the vintage department store floors of Odean Fashion Mall in under ten minutes. Hat Yai Junction Railway Station sits just a five- to eight-minute walk to the west.

### Songkhla Old Town (Nakhon Nok, Nakhon Nai & Nang Ngam)

An open-air architectural archive of century-old Sino-Portuguese rowhouses, timber lake piers, family noodle kitchens, and shaded street murals facing the inland water. It suits architecture buffs, photographers, couples, and travelers who appreciate early-morning quiet along historic alleyways. Boutique heritage rooms and renovated shophouses run between $32 and $60 USD per night. The Roo Classic Hometel offers restored heritage rooms for roughly $38 USD a night, while Club Tree Hotel balances retro styling with garden courtyards for around $35 USD. Step out the door to eat hand-churned ice cream and braised stew along Nang Ngam Road, tour the historic Hub Ho Hin Red Rice Mill, and walk past the ancient Songkhla City Gate. The Old Town Songthaew Terminus rests nearby, running continuous shuttles back toward the Hat Yai clock tower.

### Samila Beachfront (Songkhla Peninsula)

A salt-air coastal stretch shaded by towering casuarina pines, where dawn joggers, mobile coconut carts, open-air seafood tables, and open water define the daily pace. It suits families, leisurely road-trippers, and coastal wanderers who want horizon views and sea breezes rather than crowded inner-city blocks. You can step directly onto the sand to see the famous bronze Golden Mermaid Statue, pass the quirky Cat & Mouse Sculptures, wander the breezy Chalathat Beach promenade, or take an easy walk down to the Songkhla Aquarium. The open-air Samila Tram loops right through the park, meeting the main Chana-Songkhla roadway.

## Things to do

<GuidePhoto slot="activity" />

### Half-Day Excursions

**Kim Yong Morning Market & Downtown Dim Sum**

Dive into the tight, bustling aisles of this historic market between 06:30 AM and 10:00 AM to sample roasted cashews, dried local longan, southern batik textiles, and steaming bamboo stacks of pork and shrimp dumplings. Entry is free, and a hearty breakfast spread costs around ฿150 to ฿250 ($4.50–$7.50 USD). No advance reservations are needed; just show up early with an appetite.

**Hat Yai Municipal Park & Phra Phuttha Mongkol Maharaj Hilltop**

Ride Thailand’s open-air ridgeline cable car between hillsides to stand before the towering 20-meter golden standing Buddha watching over the plain below. Walking the park grounds costs nothing, while the foreign visitor cable car ticket is ฿200 ($6.00 USD). Arrive between 04:30 PM and 06:30 PM on a weekday to catch the cooler summit breeze as golden hour hits the limestone hills.

### Full-Day Excursions

**Songkhla Old Town Heritage & Coffee Walk**

Spend a full day tracing historic timber shophouses, checking out modern street art along Raman Road, touring the lakefront wooden loading platform at the Hub Ho Hin Red Rice Mill, and tasting ice cream flavored with local coconut milk. Walking the town lanes is free, while local heritage exhibits ask for an entry fee of around ฿150 ($4.50 USD). Plan this for a Friday, Saturday, or Sunday when local artisan studios and specialty coffee counters open their wooden shutters; you can book the Songkhla Old Town Heritage Walk on Klook to dig straight into the regional backstories.

**Tang Kuan Hill Cable Lift & Samila Beach Mermaid**

Take the mechanical funicular lift up Tang Kuan Hill to view the ancient Dvaravati-era stupa and the hilltop royal sala, keeping a sharp eye on the resident monkeys, before wandering down to the white sand of Samila Beach to greet the bronze Golden Mermaid. The funicular ticket costs ฿40 ($1.20 USD) for adults, and the beach is completely free. Go between 08:30 AM and 11:00 AM to explore the viewpoints before the midday coastal sun heats up the stone staircases.

**Songkhla Central Mosque & Khlong Hae Floating Market**

Catch the afternoon reflection of minarets across the calm water pool at the grand provincial mosque, often referred to as the Taj Mahal of Thailand, then travel north to Khlong Hae to watch vendors in wooden longtail boats hand up crispy fried chicken, quail eggs, and fresh herbal drinks in clay pots. The mosque and floating market grounds are free to enter, with boat snacks priced between ฿30 and ฿60 ($0.90–$1.80 USD) each. This trip works exclusively on Friday, Saturday, or Sunday starting around 03:00 PM when the boats paddle into position along the canal banks.

**Ton Nga Chang Wildlife Sanctuary & 7-Tier Elephant Tusk Waterfall**

Trek deep into the damp, dense evergreen jungle along a granite mountain trail to reach the dramatic third tier of the falls, where the rushing river splits across dark rock faces like two massive curved ivory tusks into clear, cool swimming pools. Entry to the wildlife sanctuary costs ฿200 ($6.00 USD) for foreign adults and ฿100 ($3.00 USD) for children, plus a ฿30 ($0.90 USD) parking fee for two-wheelers. Hit the trail between 09:00 AM and 12:00 PM on a Tuesday or Wednesday to enjoy the freshwater pools before local families arrive for weekend picnics.

### Multi-Day Excursions

**Songkhla Lake Basin & Thale Noi Waterbird Excursion (2 Days / 1 Night)**

Cross the elevated Ekachai Bridge across the lake marshlands into Phatthalung to board a longtail wooden boat at daybreak, skimming past thousands of open pink lotus blossoms, rare migratory waterbirds, and herds of semi-feral water buffalo feeding in the shallows. Chartering a private boat costs ฿500 to ฿600 ($15–$18 USD) per vessel, while rustic lakefront wooden homestays run ฿800 to ฿1,200 ($24–$36 USD) for the night. Aim for the peak blooming window between February and April, and make sure to reserve your waterside cabin ahead of time.

**Hat Yai to Koh Lipe Island Crossing (3 Days / 2 Nights)**

Connect the southern rail hub directly to the deep Andaman Sea by booking an early air-conditioned minivan from downtown Hat Yai to Pak Bara Pier in Satun, where a high-speed catamaran ferries you out to the brilliant coral reefs and white sandbars of Koh Lipe. A round-trip shared minivan and speedboat ticket costs ฿1,400 to ฿1,800 ($42–$54 USD), plus a mandatory ฿200 ($6.00 USD) Tarutao National Park conservation entry fee collected at the pier. High season runs from November through April when the sea is glass-flat; you can easily book the Koh Lipe Transfer on Klook ahead of your travel date to lock in ferry seats.

For a closer look at the working lake, add Hua Khao village on the Songkhla side of the basin as a separate stop. Its stilted fishing huts and lift nets show how daily life follows the water; allow time for the journey from town and confirm local access before setting out.

Hat Yai’s boxy six-seater Daihatsu tuk-tuks do not use meters; short downtown rides should cost ฿40 to ฿60 ($1.20–$1.80 USD) per passenger, so always agree on the price before climbing aboard. If you want two wheels, independent shops near the railway station rent standard scooters for ฿250 to ฿350 ($7.50–$10.50 USD) per day—just make sure you carry a valid International Driving Permit with a motorcycle endorsement.

## Local tips + scams

<GuidePhoto slot="localTips" />

### Three Local Etiquette Rules

* **Respect Spatial Halal Boundaries:** Roughly a third of the local population is Thai-Muslim. Never bring outside pork snacks, takeaway skewers, or opened beer cans into Muslim-run tea stalls (*Roti/Teh Tarik* joints) or anywhere near neighborhood prayer halls.
* **Watch the Doorstep for Shoes:** In Songkhla Old Town, traditional family pharmacies, small art studios, and third-generation noodle houses treat the front shop threshold as an extension of the family living room. Look down at the floor: if you spot a row of slip-on shoes or flip-flops parked on the tiles, slide your footwear off before stepping inside.
* **Handle Ringgit with Clean Hands:** Because of regular cross-border trade, vendors at Kim Yong and Santisuk markets readily accept Malaysian Ringgit (MYR) along with Thai Baht. Always hand over crisp paper banknotes using your right hand; never offer battered coins, crumpled bills, or throw money flat onto display trays.

### Three Active Tourist Scams

* **The "Border Run Canceled" Taxi Pitch:** Men lingering around Hat Yai Junction Railway Station often approach travelers with backpacks, insisting that all public minivans heading south to the Sadao Malaysian border have stopped running for emergency police checks. They will offer a private "express border taxi" for ฿1,500 to ฿2,000 ($45–$60 USD). Ignore them entirely; regular air-conditioned minivans run continuously from Hat Yai Bus Terminal 1 for under ฿70 ($2.10 USD).
* **Forced Temple Offerings at Tang Kuan Hill:** As you exit songthaews near Tang Kuan Hill or the hilltop shrine at Municipal Park, pushy vendors will thrust bags of dried corn kernels or yellow marigold garlands directly into your hands, nodding as if it is an included cultural custom. The moment your fingers close around the item, they demand ฿100 to ฿200 ($3.00–$6.00 USD). Keep your hands in your pockets or wave them off firmly without touching the items.
* **The Bottom-Box Dried Fruit Switch:** At dried fruit stalls around Kim Yong Market, counter displays showcase pristine, plump dried mangoes and giant roasted cashews for you to sample. Once you agree to purchase a kilo, some vendors will turn to a dark box under the counter and scoop brittle, low-grade pieces into your plastic bag. Insist politely that they bag your goods directly from the top display tray right in front of your eyes.

### The Resident Insider Tip

* **The 6:00 AM Dim Sum Two-Step:** At premier downtown dim sum breakfast institutions like Chokdee Tae Tien, sitting down and waiting for someone to bring you a menu means you will sit hungry for an hour. The local drill is completely different: walk inside, take a laminated table number card from the front desk, carry it straight to the massive open glass steamer cases, stack your chosen raw bamboo baskets onto a metal cafeteria tray, hand the tray to the kitchen steamers alongside your table card, and only then find your seat. Your tea and piping-hot dumplings will arrive at your table minutes later.

## FAQ

### Is Hat Yai and Songkhla safe for solo female travelers?

Yes, street safety is high and harassment is rare. Stick to standard personal safety habits: use Bolt or Grab rather than hailing lone tuk-tuks down quiet streets after midnight, and avoid walking along the unlit railway track paths behind the central train station alone late at night.

### How many days do I need?

Three days and two nights is the ideal timeframe. Dedicate day one to downtown Hat Yai's food alleys and Kim Yong Market, day two to Songkhla Old Town and Samila Beach, and your third morning to the hiking trails at Ton Nga Chang Waterfall and Municipal Park.

### Is Hat Yai and Songkhla expensive?

Not at all. Backpackers comfortably get by on $22 to $32 USD per day using shared songthaews and street noodle carts. Mid-range travelers spend $55 to $80 USD per day for boutique hotels, fresh seafood dinners, and air-conditioned rides, while luxury travel rarely crosses $140 to $240 USD.

### What is the best area for first-timers to stay?

Stay in Central Downtown Hat Yai, close to Lee Gardens Plaza and Kim Yong Market. You will be able to walk to dozens of famous food joints, catch songthaews right outside your door, and walk to the train station in under ten minutes without renting a scooter.

### Do I need to carry cash?

Yes, cash is completely necessary every day. Small family food stalls, morning markets, and local songthaew drivers do not accept international credit cards. Locals pay through Thai PromptPay QR codes, but overseas cards only work in large department stores, 7-Elevens, and major hotels. Carry ฿1,000 to ฿1,500 daily.

### Is it safe to travel south given regional news advisories?

Hat Yai city and Songkhla municipality are active, secure commercial hubs fully exempt from typical travel warnings. While foreign consulates advise against rural transit in the far-southern border provinces of Yala, Pattani, and Narathiwat, Hat Yai and coastal Songkhla remain welcoming and straightforward to explore.
