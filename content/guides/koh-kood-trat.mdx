*A quiet Trat island where your beach choice shapes the whole trip.*

## Quick facts

- **Where:** Koh Kood, also written Ko Kut, is an island in Thailand's Trat province, south of Koh Chang and Koh Mak. [Tourism Authority of Thailand](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Destinations/Provinces/Ko-Kut/470)
- **Main crossing:** Boats leave the Trat mainland from Laem Sok. Departure times change with the season and weather; confirm the operator's check-in point and return trip before booking onward travel. [Tourism Authority of Thailand](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Attraction/ko-kut-2)
- **Best first base:** Khlong Chao gives you a beach, river access and a convenient starting point for the waterfall. Bang Bao suits a slower beach stay; Ao Tapao is useful if you want to stay closer to the northern arrival side.
- **Pace:** Allow at least three nights if you want a beach day, a waterfall outing and a village or boat trip without turning every day into a transfer.

## When to go

The [Tourism Authority of Thailand](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Destinations/Provinces/Ko-Kut/470) recommends **November to February** for Koh Kood. Those months suit a trip built around swimming, boat outings and a more dependable ferry timetable. They also attract more visitors, so the small beachfront properties are worth reserving ahead. Ask a hotel about the exact stretch of sand in front of it: two places described as “beachfront” may have very different swimming conditions.

March and April can still work well if you are comfortable with hotter days. Plan the waterfall walk or a village visit early, then leave the exposed beach hours for shade. By the wetter months, a beautiful clear morning may turn into a rougher afternoon at sea. The tourism authority warns that low-season boat service is limited; [Boonsiri's current timetable](https://boonsiriferry.com/kh/news/update-timetable-jun2026-boonsiri-ferry) also says departures can be changed for weather and operating conditions. Check the [Trat forecast](https://www.tmd.go.th/en/weather/province/trat) and the boat operator again close to departure.

If Koh Kood is the last stop before an international flight, spend your final night on the mainland or give yourself a generous buffer. A ferry cancellation is an inconvenience when you have a spare day and a much bigger problem when it is tied to a fixed flight.

## Where to stay

### Khlong Chao: the practical first stay

Khlong Chao is the simplest base if you want more than a resort beach. Its shoreline, river and route toward Khlong Chao Waterfall give you several easy ways to divide a day. Stay near the beach for a swim before breakfast, or choose a riverside property if kayaking matters more. Check the map carefully: a room described as “Khlong Chao” may be inland, and the island is not arranged like a walkable town. The [tourism authority](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Articles/koh-kut-hideout-haven-of-the-east) highlights both the river kayaking and waterfall walk.

### Bang Bao: an easy beach afternoon

Bang Bao Bay is a good choice if you want to settle into one stretch of shore and linger through sunset. The bay has beachfront accommodation and piers, but the experience depends on where you stay: a resort's own jetty is not the same thing as a public ferry terminal. Ask about the precise beach access and whether the room faces the water. If you plan to eat outside your accommodation every night, also check how you will get home after dark.

### Ao Tapao and the northern coast

Ao Tapao works for travelers who want a broad west-coast beach and a base on the northern half of the island. It can be a practical choice after arriving at Ao Salad, though you should still confirm whether your boat ticket includes a transfer to the hotel. A few northern beaches have resort access arrangements, so check the exact public approach before setting out for a casual beach hop. For a special-occasion stay, compare what is included in the room rate rather than assuming every north-coast property has the same services.

### Ao Prao and the quieter south

The south suits you if a slow beach day matters more than quick access to several restaurants. Ao Prao is one of the beaches named in the [tourism authority's Ko Kut listing](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Attraction/ko-kut-2). Check the distance to a shop, how you will reach your chosen waterfall or village, and the departure time on your final day. A remote room can be lovely until you realize every meal and ride needs organizing.

## Things to do

### Swim at Ao Noi and compare the beaches

Give yourself a morning with no itinerary. Ao Noi's palms, boulders and turquoise shallows make a useful contrast with a broader bay such as Bang Bao. Conditions vary by tide and weather, so take a look at the water before committing to a swim. The [tourism authority](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Attraction/ko-kut-2) lists Ao Noi among the island's beaches. If you have only a short stay, enjoying two distinct beaches is more satisfying than racing around every named cove.

### Walk to Khlong Chao Waterfall

This is the inland counterpoint to your beach time. Walk in shoes that can handle wet ground and ask locally about the path and water level on the day you go. Waterfalls change dramatically across seasons, and the pool is worth enjoying only when conditions are safe. The [tourism authority](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Destinations/Provinces/Ko-Kut/470) names Namtok Khlong Chao as a Koh Kood highlight.

### Paddle Khlong Chao, then visit Ao Yai

A kayak on the Khlong Chao waterway offers a different scale from an offshore boat trip: low banks, mangroves and quiet bends. Ask the operator about the route, tide and return point, and keep a dry bag for your phone. On another day, visit Ao Yai on the island's east side. It is a working fishing village, so go for a meal and a respectful walk rather than treating the homes and boats as a staged attraction. Both the waterway and village are described by the [tourism authority](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Articles/koh-kut-hideout-haven-of-the-east).

### Look for a responsible snorkel trip

Boat operators may offer reef trips around Koh Kood and nearby islands. Ask exactly where the boat will go, whether equipment and a guide are included, and how the route changes if the sea turns. [Tourism Authority of Thailand](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Destinations/Provinces/Ko-Kut/470) lists snorkeling and trips to nearby islands among the activities here. Do not touch coral or wildlife, and do not assume an image of a sea creature proves it was photographed at the stop you will visit.

<GuidePhoto slot="marineLife" caption="A nudibranch photographed underwater; marine-life sightings vary by site and conditions." />

### Pause at Ao Salad

Ao Salad is the northern fishing village associated with the island's boat arrivals. Even if your transfer takes you straight to a hotel, it is useful to understand that the ferry pier and the village are part of a working coast. A short stop gives context to the boats and the island's daily rhythm. The [tourism authority's island overview](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Destinations/Provinces/Ko-Kut/470) mentions the village on the northeast coast.

## Getting around

Fly into Trat or travel overland, then reach **Laem Sok** for the crossing. The [tourism authority](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Attraction/ko-kut-2) lists several operators and notes that boat timetables depend on the season. As of the operator's [2026 timetable](https://boonsiriferry.com/kh/news/update-timetable-jun2026-boonsiri-ferry), Boonsiri lists three daily Laem Sok departures, but treat those as a planning reference and confirm your date and check-in instructions directly. The operator also sells some through tickets from Bangkok.

There is no public bus network around the island. [Tourism Authority of Thailand](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Articles/koh-kut-hideout-haven-of-the-east) suggests motorcycles, bicycles or arranged songthaews. If you are not comfortable on a motorbike, ask your stay to arrange a driver and agree on the return fare before setting off. Road distances are deceptive because beaches are separated by forest and hills. Arrange the ride back from a remote restaurant before sunset rather than counting on passing transport.

## Local tips + scams

Keep some baht for small restaurants and local rides, and ask your accommodation how it prefers payment. The tourism authority's older island page says ATMs are scarce, but availability can change; checking before you leave Trat is safer than relying on a particular machine. Take cash from a bank or familiar ATM on the mainland, and keep a second payment option for your hotel.

Book your **return boat** while you arrange the outbound trip. Confirm the operator, pier, hotel pickup and baggage rules in the same message. Boat companies can use different check-in points, and their schedules can move with the weather. A cheap-looking transfer that omits the road leg may cost you time and another fare.

At a beach or waterfall, pay attention to access and conditions rather than following an old photograph. Do not walk onto private resort areas assuming they are public, and do not swim in a waterfall pool during unsafe flow. At fishing villages, ask before photographing people at work. These small habits make the island easier to enjoy and keep the visit respectful.

## FAQ

### Is Koh Kood suitable for a first island trip?

Yes, if you want beaches and low-key days and are happy to plan the ferry and local rides. Khlong Chao is a useful first base because you can combine the beach, river and waterfall without changing hotels. It is less convenient if your ideal trip depends on frequent public transport or late-night venues.

### How many nights should I stay?

Three nights allow one easy beach day, one inland or village outing, and room for a weather change. Four or five nights make sense if you want a boat trip and two quite different parts of the island. For a one-night stop, the mainland transfer may take too much of your available time.

### Is it safe to travel solo?

Use the normal precautions you would on any island: share your route with someone, choose a well-reviewed stay, avoid riding an unfamiliar motorbike after dark, and check water conditions before swimming. If you travel alone, arrange return transport from remote beaches and keep your phone charged. No destination can promise a risk-free trip.

### Should I bring cash?

Bring enough Thai baht for meals, small rides and a backup day. Ask your hotel in advance whether it takes cards and whether an ATM is available near your route. Do not count on finding a machine as soon as you land; the mainland is the easier place to sort out cash before the ferry.

### Where should I stay without a motorbike?

Choose Khlong Chao for the most straightforward mix of beach and nearby activities, and book a property that can arrange pickups. Bang Bao also works if you intend to spend most of your time at one beach. Confirm how you will reach dinner and the departure pier before paying for a remote room.

### Can I visit Koh Kood in the rainy season?

You can, but build the trip around flexible plans. Some boat services are reduced and weather can change the crossing or an offshore excursion. Confirm the operator's timetable and read the Trat forecast close to travel. Leave room between the return ferry and a fixed flight, or sleep on the mainland the night before.
