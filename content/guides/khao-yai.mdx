## Quick facts

- **Location:** Khao Yai National Park lies about 205 kilometres northeast of Bangkok by the northern route. Pak Chong is the usual gateway for Mu Si and the Thanarat Road resort corridor; Noen Hom in Prachinburi serves the southern side. [Khao Yai National Park](https://khaoyainationalpark.com/en/plan-your-visit/getting-here)
- **Opening hours and entry:** The park is normally open 06:00–18:00. At the Pak Chong and Noen Hom gates, the published foreign admission is THB 400 per adult and THB 200 per child, plus THB 30 for a four-wheel vehicle. A ticket is valid for one day. Check the official page again before visiting. [Khao Yai National Park](https://www.khaoyainationalpark.com/en/plan-your-visit/getting-here/park-entry-fees)
- **Time and money:** Thailand uses ICT (UTC+7) and Thai baht. Carry cash for park services, smaller restaurants and operators that do not take cards.
- **Stay length:** Two or three nights gives you one full park day plus time for a bat cave, vineyard or slower countryside outing.
- **Entry:** Visa rules depend on your passport. Thailand revised its visa-exemption and visa-on-arrival arrangements on 15 September 2026, so check the current official list before departure. All non-Thai nationals must submit the free TDAC within three days before arrival, including the arrival date. [Thai Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/pb-summary-03092026-en) [Thailand Digital Arrival Card](https://tdac.immigration.go.th/manual/en/faq.html)

## When to go

The park identifies **October to February** as cool season, **March to May** as hot season and **June to September** as rainy season. Its published annual average is 21°C; higher areas can fall below 10°C at night in December and January. Bring a light layer for dawn and night drives. [Khao Yai National Park](https://khaoyainationalpark.com/en/about/climate)

<GuidePhoto slot="whenToGo" />

- **January:** Cool mornings and dry conditions make this an easy first-visit month. Popular weekends need early hotel and tour bookings.
- **February:** Still comfortable for trails and wildlife drives, with warmer afternoons as the cool season fades.
- **March:** Hot season begins. Start walking early and keep water in the vehicle.
- **April:** Expect heat and heavier domestic travel around Songkran on 13–15 April.
- **May:** Humidity builds and showers become more likely. Keep waterfall and trail plans flexible.
- **June:** The rainy season brings dense green forest. Confirm trail and viewpoint access before leaving your hotel.
- **July:** Rain gear and shoes with grip matter. Wildlife tours still run, but routes can change with conditions.
- **August:** A lush month with changeable weather. Avoid building the whole trip around one exposed viewpoint.
- **September:** Usually the wettest end of the park calendar. Waterfalls are powerful, and slippery paths need more care.
- **October:** The official cool season begins, though rain may linger. Check road and trail notices.
- **November:** Cooler, clearer weather returns and weekend demand rises.
- **December:** Cool nights and busy holiday dates reward advance planning.

For most first visits, compare November through February. The forest is comfortable, but rooms and tours can tighten on weekends and holidays. The rainy months suit travelers who value green scenery and strong waterfalls and can change plans around weather. The park posts annual closures separately; in 2026, its Thai entry-fee page announced the Pha Diao Dai viewpoint and nature trail closed from 1 June through 30 September. Treat that as a dated notice and verify the current closure list for every trip. [Khao Yai National Park](https://www.khaoyainationalpark.com/plan-your-visit/getting-here/park-entry-fees)

## Where to stay

Choose your transport and then your hotel. Khao Yai is a wide countryside destination, and a room described as “near the park” may still require a long drive to the entrance or visitor centre. Compare the live room rate, breakfast, cancellation terms and transfer arrangements for your dates instead of relying on a destination-wide price estimate.

### Pak Chong town

Pak Chong is the practical base for train and bus arrivals. It suits solo travelers and anyone joining a tour that collects from town. You trade resort scenery for easier logistics and local restaurants.

Rimtarn Hotel Pakchong and The Pino Hotel Pakchong are two properties to compare. Check the exact distance from the station and your operator’s pickup point before booking; “Pak Chong” covers more ground than a compact station district.

### Mu Si and Thanarat Road

Mu Si is my first choice for a first visit when you already have a car, driver or confirmed tour pickup. Thanarat Road leads toward the northern park gate and holds many of the area’s resorts, restaurants and family attractions. It shortens the approach to the park without turning the district into a walkable town.

### Nong Nam Daeng

Nong Nam Daeng works for families or small groups with their own vehicle who want countryside space south of Pak Chong. It is a base for slow mornings and resort time rather than a district you explore on foot. Watermill Resort is a practical mid-range comparison; Te Mata Glamping is a more elaborate alternative.

### Phaya Yen

Phaya Yen suits a quieter resort or vineyard-focused stay west of Pak Chong. Choose it for a couple’s countryside break or a family trip where PB Valley and time at the hotel matter as much as the national park. It is less convenient for a train arrival followed by several independent day trips.

Patravana Resort and Rain Tree Khao Yai Hotel are two properties to compare. Ask about transfers, restaurant hours and the route to any vineyard booking. Do not assume neighboring attractions are within safe walking distance.

### Full-day

3. **Join a guided wildlife day.** A guide helps with calls, tracks and park logistics, but no responsible operator can promise elephants or hornbills. 4. **Build a waterfall and viewpoint circuit.** Haew Suwat is the familiar stop; Haew Narok requires a different drive and more time. 5. **Walk a nature trail.** The park lists seven short trails from 1.2 to 8 kilometres, with estimated walking times from 45 minutes to six hours. Some can be walked independently, while route conditions and guide requirements vary. Ask the visitor centre which trail is open and suitable that day. [Khao Yai National Park](https://www.khaoyainationalpark.com/en/plan-your-visit/thing-to-do)

6. **Add the official night safari.** Reserve and pay at the visitor centre before 18:00. The park runs one-hour departures at 19:00 and 20:00 in ranger vehicles; the published charge is THB 600 per vehicle for up to eight people. ## Getting around

From Bangkok, drive, hire a driver, join a tour or travel by rail or bus to Pak Chong. Public transport reaches the gateway but does not provide a sightseeing network inside the park. [Khao Yai National Park](https://khaoyainationalpark.com/en/plan-your-visit/getting-here)

If you stay in Pak Chong, ask your tour whether station pickup is included. Greenleaf says it provides free transfers to and from the Pak Chong train or bus station when you take at least its full-day tour. For a private arrival, compare the pickup address, vehicle size, tolls, waiting time and final resort drop-off before paying. ## Local tips + scams

<GuidePhoto slot="localTips" />

Inside the park, keep noise low and take litter out with you. Do not feed or touch wildlife. Pets and alcohol are prohibited, and drone use needs permission. Waterfall barriers and ranger instructions take priority over the photograph you hoped to make. [Khao Yai National Park](https://www.khaoyainationalpark.com/en/plan-your-visit/visitor-safety-and-regulations)

If an elephant blocks the road, stop at least 30 metres away, remain in the vehicle and keep the engine running. Do not honk or use flash. Reverse carefully if it approaches and leave it a clear route. The park’s instructions should override advice from another driver. [Khao Yai National Park](https://www.khaoyainationalpark.com/en/plan-your-visit/visitor-safety-and-regulations)

<GuidePhoto slot="elephantSafety" />

At temples and shrines, cover shoulders and knees where requested, remove shoes when signs or hosts ask and avoid pointing your feet at Buddha images. Speak quietly with local staff and drivers. Do not buy or consume wildlife products; leave animals and forest plants where they belong.

Get the full transport or tour price, pickup point, park-fee inclusion, cancellation terms and return time in writing. Photograph a rental before leaving and do not hand over your passport as security. Use bank ATMs and keep your card in sight.

At Pak Chong Night Market, carry small notes and confirm the price before ordering.

<GuidePhoto slot="pakChongFood" />

Use only the official TDAC website linked above. The form is free, so a payment request comes from a third-party service rather than Thailand Immigration. Save your hotel name and address before opening the form; it asks for accommodation details. [Thailand Digital Arrival Card](https://tdac.immigration.go.th/manual/en/faq.html)

## FAQ

### Is Khao Yai suitable for a solo female trip?

Yes, with firm transport arrangements. Pak Chong works well if a reputable tour collects you from town. Avoid relying on an unconfirmed late return from a remote resort, share the day’s route with someone you trust and use the same normal drink, valuables and licensed-driver precautions you would elsewhere in Thailand.

### How many days do I need?

Allow two or three nights. One full day can cover a guided park visit, while the remaining time fits a bat cave, vineyard or resort afternoon. A longer stay gives you room to change a trail or waterfall plan when rain, closures or wildlife conditions alter the day.

### Is Khao Yai expensive?

Transport creates the largest difference. Park entry is published, but a train-and-group-tour trip costs far less than a private driver and high-end resort. Price the hotel, Bangkok transfer, park day and one extra activity for your actual dates, then keep a reserve for meals and changed transport.

### Where should first-time visitors stay?

Choose Mu Si or Thanarat Road when you have a car, driver or confirmed pickup and want the northern park approach. Choose Pak Chong when rail or bus arrival and tour logistics matter more than resort atmosphere. Neither area removes the need to plan movement inside the national park.

### Do I need cash?

Yes. The park and some operators may accept particular payment methods, but smaller restaurants and local services often work more easily in cash. Greenleaf currently states that its guesthouse takes cash only. Carry enough baht for the day, keep your larger reserve separate and confirm payment directly.

### What should I do if an elephant blocks the road?

Stop at least 30 metres away, remain inside, keep the engine running and give the animal a clear route. Do not sound the horn or use flash. If it comes closer, reverse slowly when safe. Follow ranger instructions and never approach for a photograph. [Khao Yai National Park](https://www.khaoyainationalpark.com/en/plan-your-visit/visitor-safety-and-regulations)
