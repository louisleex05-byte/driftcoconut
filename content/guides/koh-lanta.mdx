## Quick facts

- **Gateway airport:** Koh Lanta has no commercial airport. Fly into Krabi International Airport (**KBV**), then continue by road and vehicle ferry to Koh Lanta Yai. [Visit Thailand Today](https://www.visitthailandtoday.com/guides/krabi-to-koh-lanta)
- **Currency:** Thai baht (**฿ / THB**). For September 2026 planning, use around **฿33.3 = US$1**, then check the live rate before you exchange. [Bank of Thailand](https://app.bot.or.th/BTWS_STAT/statistics/BOTWEBSTAT.aspx?reportID=123&language=eng)
- **Time:** ICT, UTC+7, all year. Thailand does not use daylight saving time.
- **Language:** Thai is the everyday language. English works at resorts, dive shops, tour desks, Saladan, and the main west-coast beach strips, but it is less reliable in small local businesses.
- **Visa note:** From 15 September 2026, US, UK, Australian, Japanese, and listed EU passport holders can use Thailand’s 30-day visa-exemption scheme for tourism, subject to immigration requirements. This is not a visa on arrival. [Royal Thai Embassy Helsinki](https://helsinki.thaiembassy.org/en/publicservice/updated-tourist-visa-exemption-scheme-30-days-effective-15-september-2?cate=5d80865815e39c3354007650)
- **Daily mid-range spend:** Plan **฿3,100–5,100 (US$93–153)** for a room, three meals, and local transport, before boat tours, diving, drinking, and private transfers. [Thailand Journey](https://thailandjourney.org/koh-lanta-travel-guide-beaches-culture-and-ethical-tourism/)
- **Getting around:** No metro, BTS, rail line, or dependable island-wide bus network. Scooter, arranged transfer, tuk-tuk/taxi, and occasional songthaew are the practical options. [Lanta Nomads](https://lantanomads.com/transportation/)

## When to go

January and February are Koh Lanta at its most settled. January averages 32.6°C by day and 24.1°C at night, with about 31 mm of rain and roughly three rainy days. February is drier still: 33.5°C/24.4°C, around 24 mm of rain, and about two rainy days. Sea conditions are usually at their most dependable for Koh Rok, Koh Haa, the Four Islands, and dive trips. This is also peak season, so beach resorts, ferries, airport vans, and popular boat trips carry their highest demand. [Siam Good](https://siamgood.com/koh-lanta/best-time-to-visit/)

March remains dry enough for a very good trip, although it is hotter: average highs reach 33.9°C. April brings more heat, more afternoon storms, and Songkran crowds. Thailand’s New Year water festival falls on **13–15 April 2026** and **13–15 April 2027**, so expect travel demand and tighter hotel availability around those dates. April rainfall averages about 122 mm, so it can be a useful compromise if you want lower prices than February without committing to full monsoon conditions. [Siam Good](https://siamgood.com/koh-lanta/best-time-to-visit/)

May is the turn. Rain rises to about 228 mm, then sits near 286–342 mm from June through August. Hotels become markedly cheaper, beaches empty out, and the island slows down. That can suit a long stay if you are content with rain breaks, cafés, reading time, and flexible plans. It is not the right season to fly in for a rigid two-day island-hopping itinerary: rough seas can change routes or cancel boats. [Siam Good](https://siamgood.com/koh-lanta/best-time-to-visit/)

September and October are the wettest-feeling months. September averages 319 mm of rain and around 17 rainy days; October has the highest rainy-day count at about 18 days. You can find very good room rates, but treat clear-water snorkeling as a bonus rather than a promise. [Siam Good](https://siamgood.com/koh-lanta/best-time-to-visit/)

November marks the return. Rainfall drops to about 148 mm, operators restart more routes, and prices start climbing. Loy Krathong falls on **24 November 2026**; the expected 2027 date is **14 November**, though local observance can vary with the lunar calendar. December is dry again, with around 65 mm of rain, and Christmas–New Year pushes beachfront room rates back into peak territory. [Siam Good](https://siamgood.com/koh-lanta/best-time-to-visit/)

Koh Lanta is not in northern Thailand’s burning-season hotspot, so February–April haze is not the main reason to avoid it. The more relevant seasonal concern is dengue and other mosquito-borne illness, particularly in wetter months. Pack repellent and choose screened or air-conditioned accommodation where possible. [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/asia/thailand)

## Where to stay

### Klong Dao Beach

Klong Dao is a wide, shallow northern beach with an easy rhythm: sunrise walks, family-friendly swimming, resort restaurants, and relatively short transfers from Saladan Pier. It works particularly well if you are arriving with children, prefer a calmer shoreline, or do not want to ride a scooter every day. [Expedia](https://www.expedia.com/Ko-Lanta-Hotels.d6035252.Travel-Guide-Hotels)

Mid-range rooms generally sit around **฿1,800–2,300 (US$54–69)**, with higher rates in December through February. Southern Lanta Resort is a regular mid-range choice, with rates from around ฿1,800 in the research data. Royal Lanta Resort & Spa is another beachfront option near Klong Dao and the Long Beach edge; check live dates because rates change sharply by season. [Out Thailand](https://outthailand.com/guide/where-to-stay-koh-lanta/)

You can walk to beach restaurants, small convenience shops, casual sunset spots, and family-oriented resort facilities. Saladan Pier is about 3 km away, generally five to ten minutes by road. There is no BTS, metro, or proper station here; Saladan Pier is the only transport reference point worth using. [Out Thailand](https://outthailand.com/guide/where-to-stay-koh-lanta/)

### Long Beach / Phra Ae

Long Beach is the most balanced place to base yourself on Koh Lanta. It has a long sandy shoreline, a fuller run of cafés, beach bars, massage shops, casual restaurants, minimarts, and more accommodation choices than most of the island. You can spend the afternoon on the beach, eat dinner nearby, and still have enough around you to avoid riding out for every meal. [Out Thailand](https://outthailand.com/guide/where-to-stay-koh-lanta/)

It suits a first Koh Lanta trip, couples, solo travelers who want restaurants within reach, remote workers staying longer, and groups whose budgets are not identical. Typical rooms span **฿1,200–3,300 (US$36–99)**, depending heavily on season and whether you are steps from the sand. [Out Thailand](https://outthailand.com/guide/where-to-stay-koh-lanta/)

Long Beach Chalet is a well-known choice, located about a seven-minute walk from Long Beach according to its accommodation listing. Lazy Days Bungalows is another named option in the Phra Ae area. Neither property should be published with a fixed nightly rate without a live booking-date check. [Travelocity](https://www.travelocity.com/Ko-Lanta-Hotels-Long-Beach-Chalet.h15697370.Hotel-Information)

Saladan Pier lies around 5 km north. Plan on a taxi, tuk-tuk, arranged hotel transfer, or scooter rather than a scheduled bus. [Destination Thailand](https://destinationthailand.io/where-to-stay-in-koh-lanta/)

### Klong Khong

Klong Khong has a slower, less polished feel than Long Beach. The west-coast strip is known for lower-cost bungalows, cafés, simple Thai and international food, and sunset bars where the evening is more about sitting down than dressing up. The beach becomes rocky at low tide, so it is less straightforward for swimming than Klong Dao or Long Beach. [Destination Thailand](https://destinationthailand.io/where-to-stay-in-koh-lanta/)

Stay here if price matters, if you are planning a longer island stay, or if you want a social but unforced atmosphere. Mid-range and budget rooms run around **฿600–1,800 (US$18–54)**. Lanta New Beach Bungalows is a named local option; its live rate fluctuates, so use current booking results instead of a permanent “from” price. [HotelsCombined](https://www.hotelscombined.com/Hotel/Lanta_New_Beach_Bungalows.htm)

You can walk to beach bars, modest restaurants, small cafés, and sunset viewpoints. Saladan Pier is about 9 km away, and there is no formal transport station. Arrange rides ahead, particularly after dark. [Destination Thailand](https://destinationthailand.io/where-to-stay-in-koh-lanta/)

### Kantiang Bay

Kantiang Bay is the southern choice: more space, more views, less nightlife, and a stronger resort feel. The bay is a good fit if you want a quieter base for beach time, national park visits, and long breakfasts rather than frequent trips up to Saladan. It is especially suited to couples and travelers booking a higher-end stay. 

<GuidePhoto slot="localFood" />

Allow around **฿2,500–6,000+ (US$75–180+)** in high season, though this is a planning range rather than a fixed market rate. Pimalai Resort & Spa and The Houben Hotel are the major named options from the research, but confirm current dates before displaying an exact price.

You can walk to the bay, a small cluster of restaurants and bars, and resort facilities. You cannot walk to wide shopping or island nightlife, and Saladan is roughly 20–25 km north. Book a transfer, use a scooter only if you are confident, or plan on taxis for longer trips.

## Things to do

### Half-day

**Mu Ko Lanta National Park and Cape Ta Nod** gives you rainforest paths, a lighthouse viewpoint, and the southern end of the island in one easy outing. Foreign adult entry is **฿200 (US$6.01)**, plus ฿20 for a scooter or ฿30 for a car/light truck. Go early or later in the afternoon; the midday heat is unforgiving. [Thailand Guide](https://thailandguide.co.uk/attractions/krabi/mu-koh-lanta-national-park/)

<GuidePhoto slot="waterfall" />

**Lanta Old Town and the east coast** is the island’s quieter counterpoint to the beach road. Ride over for stilted waterfront buildings, local seafood, cafés, and a slower village pace. There is no general admission charge; budget **฿200–300 (US$6–9)** for a scooter, plus roughly **฿100–150 (US$3–5)** of fuel for an island day. ### Multi-day excursions

**A PADI Open Water course** turns Koh Lanta into more than a beach stop. Courses require several days of classroom or e-learning, confined-water work, and open-water dives. Prices vary too much by school, inclusions, and season to quote responsibly here; seek a live quote from an SSI- or PADI-affiliated operator, and plan it for November–April.

**Koh Lanta to Koh Phi Phi or Phuket** works well if you want a multi-island route rather than a return flight from Krabi. A Koh Lanta–Krabi ferry listing begins around **US$28** for about 2.5 hours, but routes to Phi Phi and Phuket vary by season. Book ahead in dry season and never assume a direct wet-season boat will run.

## Getting around

From Krabi International Airport, a shared minivan to Koh Lanta typically costs **฿350–450 (US$11–14)** and takes about two to three hours, including the vehicle-ferry crossings. A private car or minivan is usually **฿2,000–3,000 (US$60–90)** and makes sense with family, surfboards, heavy bags, or a late arrival. [Visit Thailand Today](https://www.visitthailandtoday.com/guides/krabi-to-koh-lanta)

If your flight lands late, do not assume the last shared van will wait. **Book <AffiliateLink type="welcomePickups">Welcome Pickups</AffiliateLink> ahead** or stay in Krabi overnight if the timing is tight. One 2026 guide notes that last airport minivans can depart around 13:00, though this must be confirmed with your operator. [Out Thailand](https://outthailand.com/guide/bangkok-to-koh-lanta/)

On Koh Lanta, scooter rental is about **฿200–300 (US$6–9)** daily, plus fuel. Taxis and tuk-tuks are negotiated: expect roughly **฿100–300 (US$3–9)** for short trips and **฿400–700+ (US$12–21+)** for a longer Saladan-to-Kantiang run. Songthaews cost around **฿50–100 (US$1.50–3)** where available, but they are not a dependable network. [Baanlyy](https://baanlyy.com/thailand/koh-lanta/getting-around)

## Local tips + scams

Keep your feet low in Koh Lanta. In Thailand, feet are not used to point at people, sacred objects, or furniture. Do not put them on tables, do not angle the soles toward a Buddha image, and do not step over someone sitting on the floor.

Shoes come off before entering temples and often private homes. For temples, government offices, and private homes, cover your shoulders and knees. A beach cover-up is not enough for a temple stop after lunch.

Be careful with heads as well. Avoid touching anyone’s head, including children, unless you know them well. Treat royal images with respect; Thailand’s rules and social expectations around the monarchy are serious.

For scooters, film the bike from every side before you leave the rental counter. Record scratches, cracked panels, mirrors, tire condition, and fuel level. Damage disputes are avoidable when both sides have the same visual record. Ask about deposit and insurance before handing over money.

For taxis and tuk-tuks, agree the total fare before sitting down. There are few metered rides here, Grab and Bolt are unreliable in some areas, and “short ride” pricing can become unclear once you add luggage or a return wait.

For boat trips in monsoon season, ask what happens if the captain changes the route or cancels because of sea conditions. Get refund or rebooking terms in writing. A Four Islands itinerary is not a weather guarantee.

One practical local tip: do not book a Krabi flight that lands late and expect a casual same-day minivan connection. Some airport vans leave around early afternoon, and ferry queues or rain can stretch the journey. A Krabi overnight is often less stressful than trying to force the crossing. [Out Thailand](https://outthailand.com/guide/bangkok-to-koh-lanta/)

Finally, eat carefully. Skip raw fish and wild-animal dishes, especially where storage and preparation are unclear. Choose food that is properly cooked and served hot, and keep hand hygiene simple but consistent.

## FAQ

### Is Koh Lanta safe for solo female travelers?

Yes, Koh Lanta is generally suitable for solo female travelers when you use ordinary island precautions. Choose a reviewed stay, avoid isolated stretches of beach alone after dark, watch your drink, agree transport fares before leaving, and do not ride a scooter beyond your experience. Road accidents and mosquitoes are more practical concerns than violent crime. [Visit Thailand Today](https://www.visitthailandtoday.com/guides/is-thailand-safe)

### How many days do I need in Koh Lanta?

Plan four or five nights for a first trip. That gives you an arrival day, a west-coast or Old Town day, one or two boat days, and a buffer for rain or rough seas. Three nights can work in dry season, but it leaves little room for a cancelled snorkeling trip.

### Is Koh Lanta expensive?

It can be very affordable, but island logistics add up. A backpacker can spend around **US$35–55** daily, while a practical mid-range stay is **US$93–153** before boat trips and alcohol. Peak-season beachfront hotels and private transfers raise costs quickly; May–October brings lower room rates. [Thailand Journey](https://thailandjourney.org/koh-lanta-travel-guide-beaches-culture-and-ethical-tourism/)

### What is the best area for first-time visitors?

Choose Long Beach if you want the best mix of beach, cafés, restaurants, bars, massage shops, and room choices. Pick Klong Dao instead if you are traveling with children, want calmer shallow water, or prefer to stay closer to Saladan Pier. [Out Thailand](https://outthailand.com/guide/where-to-stay-koh-lanta/)

### Do I need cash in Koh Lanta?

Yes. Resorts and some established restaurants accept cards, but scooters, songthaews, small restaurants, markets, tips, and some tour extras are cash-first. Carry Thai baht in small notes, keep a backup card, and do not rely on app-based payments or ride-hailing coverage outside the main strips.

### Can I visit Koh Lanta during monsoon season?

You can, but change the purpose of the trip. May through October is good for lower hotel rates, quiet beaches, slower days, and flexible work stays. It is less reliable for Koh Rok, Koh Haa, Emerald Cave, and fixed island-hopping schedules, particularly in August through October. [Siam Good](https://siamgood.com/koh-lanta/best-time-to-visit/)
